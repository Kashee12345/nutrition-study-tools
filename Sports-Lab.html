Part 1: Exercise Fuel Simulator

Teaching modelA simplified model of one 70 kg athlete's muscles during exercise. It shows how intensity, duration, training and fueling shape the fuel mix and glycogen stores. Real people vary a lot; this is not a training or diet plan.

MeasureValue

How the model works: energy use comes from intensity (% of VO2max) and fitness for a 70 kg athlete (about 5 kcal per litre of oxygen). The carbohydrate share follows a crossover curve that moves to higher intensities with training. Carbohydrate comes from muscle glycogen and blood glucose; the liver and any carbohydrate eaten during exercise keep blood glucose supplied. Starting stores: glycogen in the working leg muscles about 250 to 380 g depending on training (whole-body muscle holds more; about 35% more after loading); liver glycogen about 55 g after an overnight fast, 90 g after a meal. The athlete fatigues when muscle glycogen runs very low, when the liver can no longer keep blood glucose up, or, above about 80% VO2max, from fast-system by-products. Simplified for learning.

    Part 2: Sweat and Hydration Bench

    Bench readouts at the selected time
    MeasureValue

    A teaching model. Sweat rate rises with intensity, temperature and humidity (typical range about 0.3 to 2.5 L per hour). One litre of sweat is about 1 kg of body mass. Sweat sodium is set at about 900 mg per litre (1,500 mg for a salty sweater). Blood sodium is estimated from total body water (about 60% of body mass) and sodium gained or lost; core temperature rises with intensity, heat, humidity and dehydration. Real people vary widely.
